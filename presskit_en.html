>> AUTHOR

John Castle is a Chilean author and composer who builds universes at the intersection of speculative fiction and music. His work makes no distinction between formats: a book, an album and a map are parts of the same territory.

His Z3RO trilogy — published under his independent imprint Ediciones Castle — explores what happens when a corporation tries to own a consciousness. Three standalone stories converging on a single system, written for readers of Black Mirror, Ted Chiang and Kazuo Ishiguro.

As a solo composer, he develops several projects in parallel — from cinematic sci-fi scoring to synthwave, ambient and minimalist piano. His synthwave project In The Haze has surpassed 200,000 streams on Spotify, was featured in volume 3 of Rockaxis's “Nuevos Sonidos Chilenos” (New Chilean Sounds), reaches listeners in the United Kingdom, Italy, Sweden and Australia, and has been highlighted on Germany's Dark Tunnel Radio. Dystopian Voyager, his cinematic sci-fi project, has accumulated over 100,000 streams on Spotify and is the author of the official Z3RO soundtrack, released three days before the books.

Based in Santiago, Chile.

His visual work “Espejo de los Dioses” (“Mirror of the Gods”) was selected as one of the 20 finalist works of the 1st “Chile through Art and Artificial Intelligence” Contest, organized by the Cultural Outreach Office of Universidad Andrés Bello (2026).

READ THE ARTIST'S STATEMENT
Mirror of the Gods: The Fragility of Reflection

The work problematizes Chile's territorial memory: those landscapes of the south that we inhabit, photograph and love, but whose names the urgency of the present sometimes keeps us from retaining. I position myself from a personal, emotional point of view — that of someone who once stood before that lake, captured its light, and years later wonders whether it will still be the same.

Inspired by J.M.W. Turner, whose painting turns nature into omen, I found in his language the only honest way to express the sublime and the fragile at once.

The horizon is the philosophical heart of the work: that imperceptible line where photography dies and painting is born, where the real fractures into the imagined. It is not a limit — it is a threshold. It is exactly there that Chile can still choose what it will become. Turner understood that the horizon does not separate land and sky, but the present from destiny.

Two visual elements articulate this tension: the pier, a modest human trace that enters the water like someone asking permission; and the sky in flames, a painterly storm that threatens to devour the calm of the reflection.

The tool used was Gemini, running Google's Imagen 3 model. Significantly, the watermark that Imagen 3 stamps on generated images ended up naturally covered by the real photograph laid over it — as if the work itself confirmed its own thesis: the authentic prevails over the fabricated. We dream of a Chile where a nameless lake remains a mirror of something sacred.
